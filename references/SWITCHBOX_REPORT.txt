 1.  WHY THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS
===============================================================================

There are three relay cards in each bench's HP 75000 Series B (E1300A)
mainframe. They look interchangeable from GPIB - all three answer *IDN? with
the identical string:

    HEWLETT-PACKARD,SWITCHBOX,0,A.06.00                             [MEASURED]

They are not interchangeable. Two different card families are in use, they
behave differently, and the same GPIB secondary address holds a DIFFERENT card
depending on which bench is plugged in. Closing "channel 3" without knowing
which card answered is how you energise something you did not intend.

The only reliable identification is SYST:CTYP?, per slot.                [MEASURED]

===============================================================================
 3.  HOW A CARD IS ADDRESSED
===============================================================================

Three nested levels. Getting any of them wrong silently talks to the wrong
thing.

    GPIB primary 9
      |
      +-- secondary address  =  VXI logical address / 8              [MEASURED]
      |     e.g. LADDR 120 -> secondary 15 -> GPIB0::9::15::INSTR
      |
      +-- card number within the switchbox (1..8)
      |     lowest logical address in the group is card 01           [MANUAL]
      |     every card here is alone in its group, so card = 1       [MEASURED]
      |
      +-- channel number 00..15  (plus 90/91/92 on multiplexers)

Channel list syntax:

    (@ccnn)     cc = card number, nn = channel      e.g. (@0103) or (@103)
                Leading zero on the card may be dropped: "close channel 02 of
                card 1, send 102 instead of 0102".                    [MANUAL]
                Both forms verified accepted.                       [MEASURED]

    Tree switches are addressed the same way: (@190) is card 1, channel 90.


===============================================================================
 4.  CARD FAMILY A - RELAY MULTIPLEXERS (E1343A / E1345A)
===============================================================================

Each channel carries THREE lines: High (H), Low (L), Guard (G).       [MANUAL]
Sixteen channels, split into two banks:

        Bank 0  =  channels 00 .. 07
        Bank 1  =  channels 08 .. 15                                  [MANUAL]

THE PART THAT CATCHES PEOPLE OUT: closing a channel connects it only to its
bank common. It reaches the outside world through a TREE SWITCH, and the tree
switch is itself an addressable channel. Close a channel without its tree
switch and you have connected nothing.

        channel 90  =  AT  tree switch
        channel 91  =  BT  tree switch
        channel 92  =  AT2 tree switch                                [MANUAL]
                        all three verified to close and read back   [MEASURED]

  BLOCK DIAGRAM
  -------------

                                                       ANALOG BUS CONNECTOR
    ch00 --+                                           (ribbon to the E1326B)
    ch01 --+                                                  |
     ...   +--- Bank 0 common ---[ AT  ch90 ]--- AT terms ----+--  H  L  G
    ch07 --+                          |                           (SENSE pair)
                                      |
                                 [ AT2 ch92 ]
                                      |
    ch08 --+                          |
    ch09 --+                          |
     ...   +--- Bank 1 common ---[ BT  ch91 ]--- BT terms ----+--  I+ I- IG
    ch15 --+                                                      (SOURCE pair)

    Every common line, tree switch line and analog bus line carries a
    100 ohm series resistor for relay protection.                     [MANUAL]
    That resistance IS in your 2-wire measurement. It does not matter at
    megohms; it matters a great deal at single ohms.

  WHAT EACH TREE SWITCH GIVES YOU
  -------------------------------

    ch90  AT    Bank 0 channels  ->  AT terminals  AND  analog bus H / L / G
    ch91  BT    Bank 1 channels  ->  BT terminals  AND  analog bus I+ / I- / IG
    ch92  AT2   Bank 1 channels  ->  AT terminals                     [MANUAL]

  So the analog bus splits the card in half: Bank 0 is the SENSE side, Bank 1
  is the SOURCE side. That is exactly the pairing the multimeter expects, and
  it is why 4-wire scanning pairs channel N with channel N+8.

  SELECTING ONE DEVICE, 2-WIRE
  ----------------------------

      ROUT:CLOS (@190)        close the AT tree switch
      ROUT:CLOS (@103)        close channel 03  (must be 00..07)

      device on channel 03  --H/L-->  Bank 0 common  -->  AT terminals
                                                     -->  analog bus H/L

  SELECTING ONE DEVICE, 4-WIRE (KELVIN)
  -------------------------------------

      ROUT:CLOS (@190)        AT tree  - sense path
      ROUT:CLOS (@191)        BT tree  - source path
      ROUT:CLOS (@103)        sense channel  N        (Bank 0)
      ROUT:CLOS (@111)        source channel N+8      (Bank 1)

      N and N+8 must be the same device, wired to both channels. This is the
      pairing SCAN:MODE FRES uses.                                    [MANUAL]
      All four closures verified accepted together.                 [MEASURED]


===============================================================================
 5.  CARD FAMILY B - FORM C SWITCH (E1364A)
===============================================================================

Completely different animal. Sixteen INDEPENDENT single-pole double-throw
relays. No banks, no tree switches, no analog bus.                    [MANUAL]

Each channel has three terminals:

        C    Common   - the moving contact
        NO   Normally Open
        NC   Normally Closed

                    NC  o------.
                               |
        C  o--------- /        +-- relay coil energised = C to NO
                       \       |   de-energised         = C to NC
                    NO  o------'

        CLOSE the channel  ->  C connects to NO                       [MANUAL]
        OPEN  the channel  ->  C connects to NC                       [MANUAL]
        A reset connects every C to its NC.                           [MANUAL]

  *** THE RELAYS ARE LATCHING ***                                     [MANUAL]

    "the relay remains in the last state during power-up or power-down"

    The card comes back from a power cycle holding whatever it was left in.
    All-open is the POST-RESET state, not the power-on state. Never assume
    the state of a form C card you have not just reset.

  RATINGS, per channel                                                [MANUAL]

    250 Vdc or 250 Vac peak (177 Vac RMS)
    1 A dc or ac RMS, non-inductive
    30 W or 40 VA maximum
    ~15 ms closure time  ->  ceiling of roughly 50 Hz scanning

  All 16 channels on probe03's wired card were exercised individually and
  read back correctly, 55 ms per close/read/open/read cycle.         [MEASURED]

    CLOS/OPEN also accept the bare form: CLOS (@1nn) without ROUT:  [MEASURED]

  IDENTITY AND STATE
    *IDN?                     -> HEWLETT-PACKARD,SWITCHBOX,0,A.06.00
                                 identical on all three cards       [MEASURED]
    SYST:CTYP? <slot>         -> the actual card type. THE ONLY WAY
                                 to tell the cards apart.           [MEASURED]
    SYST:ERR?                 -> one error, clearing as it goes     [MEASURED]
    SYST:VERS?                -> 1990.0  (SCPI version)             [MEASURED]

  SCANNING
    ROUT:SCAN:PORT ABUS       route scanned channels to analog bus  [MEASURED]
    ROUT:SCAN:PORT NONE       default; no analog bus routing        [MEASURED]
    ROUT:SCAN:PORT?           -> ABUS | NONE                        [MEASURED]
    TRIG:SOUR?                -> IMM at reset                       [MEASURED]
    INIT:CONT?                -> 0 at reset                         [MEASURED]

  NOT SUPPORTED on this firmware - do not waste time on them:
    ROUT:SCAN?                -> -113 "Undefined header"            [MEASURED]
    ROUT:FUNC?                -> -109 "Missing parameter"           [MEASURED]

  MAINFRAME (secondary address 0, not the cards)
    VXI:CONF:NUMB?            how many modules on the backplane     [MEASURED]
    VXI:CONF:DLAD?            their logical addresses               [MEASURED]
    VXI:READ? <laddr>,<off>   read a module register                [MEASURED]
    VXI:WRITE <laddr>,<off>,<val>   write one  -- SEE SECTION 10    [MEASURED]

  VXI:CONF:DLAD? is the authoritative inventory. A module that is present but
  unassigned appears there and NOWHERE else - VISA will not even open its
  address. A blind address sweep cannot find it.                     [MEASURED]


===============================================================================
 7.  WIRING AN ACTUAL MEASUREMENT
===============================================================================

  The test being reproduced (from the .PMA recipes and LaMP's own tables):
  apply 10 V, measure leakage current, to verify electrical isolation.
  Recipe parameters: Voltage=10, MeterRange=1e-4, MeterCurrentLimit=1e-6,
  NPLC=1.                                                            [MEASURED]

  OPTION A - Keithley 2400 through a multiplexer   (recommended)
  -------------------------------------------------------------
  One instrument sources and measures. It is what LaMP used
  (fldIsLampElectrical = TRUE, alongside RELAY1 and the 2001X).

      2400 FORCE HI ------> AT tree switch terminal  H
      2400 FORCE LO ------> AT tree switch terminal  L

      per die N (N = 0..7):
          ROUT:CLOS (@190)          AT tree
          ROUT:CLOS (@10N)          the die's channel
          :SOUR:FUNC VOLT ; :SOUR:VOLT 10
          :SENS:FUNC 'CURR' ; :SENS:CURR:PROT 1E-6
          :OUTP ON ; :READ?

      Sixteen channels = up to 16 two-terminal devices per card, selected one
      at a time. Far cleaner than the form C arrangement in section 8.

  OPTION B - E1326B over the analog bus
  -------------------------------------
      The multiplexer's analog bus connector cables straight to the E1326B,
      no external wiring. Bank 0 -> sense, Bank 1 -> source.
      *** On probe02 this cable is NOT FITTED. See section 10. ***  [MEASURED]

      E1326B ceiling is 1.048 Mohm, which an isolation reading will exceed.

  OPTION C - HP 3458A
  -------------------
      Its ohms functions SOURCE a known current and read the resulting
      voltage. Ranges to 1.2 Gohm - about a thousand times the E1326B's
      headroom, and the right tool if you want resistance rather than
      leakage-at-10-V.                                                [MANUAL]

          range      current sourced        range      current sourced
          10 ohm         10 mA              1 Mohm         5 uA
          100 ohm         1 mA              10 Mohm      500 nA
          1 kohm          1 mA              100 Mohm     500 nA
          10 kohm       100 uA              1 Gohm       500 nA
          100 kohm       50 uA

      It cannot be commanded to 10 V - it is a current source. If the bias
      voltage matters for comparability with LaMP's data, use option A.

      Its front/rear Terminals switch is MECHANICAL and cannot be set over
      GPIB; TERM? reports it.  probe02 reads REAR, probe03 reads FRONT. [MEASURED]

 8.  probe03's FORM C WIRING (THE 2x2 DIE MAP)
===============================================================================

  Transcribed in references/probe03mapping, encoded in
  instruments/hp_switchbox.py. Card is the E1364A at 9::10.

  The prober lands on a 2x2 shot; the relays then select each of the four dies
  in turn, two pins per die, eight coax in total.

    die   channels      coax        lands on
    -------------------------------------------------------
     1    CH00 CH01     3, 4        Input HI  / Input LO
     2    CH02 CH03     5, 6        Input HI  / Input LO
     3    CH08 CH09     7, 8        Current HI / Current LO
     4    CH10 CH11     9, 10       Current HI / Current LO

    HI-side channels: 00, 02, 08, 10   (coax 3, 5, 7, 9)
    LO-side channels: 01, 03, 09, 11   (coax 4, 6, 8, 10)
    Channels 04-07 and 12-14 are unwired spares.

  EVERY NC TERMINAL IS DAISY-CHAINED TO GROUND via CH15 NC. So an OPEN channel
  GROUNDS its probe pin. All-open is a guarded state, not a floating one:

      CH00 NC - CH01 NC - CH02 NC - CH03 NC - CH11 NC - CH10 NC
               - CH09 NC - CH08 NC - CH15 NC ---> ground

  OPERATING RULE: one HI channel and one LO channel closed at a time.
  Two HI closed at once shorts two probe pins through the HI terminal.
  instruments/hp_switchbox.py refuses this rather than trusting the caller.

  UNRESOLVED for this bench: two pins per die means the current has to be
  forced down the same pair the voltage is read from, so Input HI must be
  commoned with Current HI (and Input LO with Current LO). probe03mapping
  records no such strap. Without it, dies 1-2 can only be sensed and dies 3-4
  only driven, and no die is measurable.                              [INFERRED]
  One continuity check at the adapter settles it.


===============================================================================
 8B. probe02's MULTIPLEXER WIRING (MEASURED ON THE BENCH)
===============================================================================

  Card is the E1345A at 9::15 (LADDR 120) - RELAY1, the one LaMP's own
  tblInstruments flags fldIsLampElectrical=True. The other two cards on this
  bench (E1343A at 9::9, E1364A at 9::14) are present but were confirmed
  unused by that application, and nothing was found wired to them.

  ONLY FOUR CHANNELS GO ANYWHERE: CH00, CH01, CH02, CH03.       [MEASURED]

    channel   die of the 2x2 shot   LaMP fldSwitch   added at 10 V
    ----------------------------------------------------------------
     CH00            1                    1           ~155 pA   64 GOhm
     CH01            2                    2           ~148 pA   67 GOhm
     CH02            3                    3           ~155 pA   65 GOhm
     CH03            4                    4           ~101 pA   99 GOhm
     CH04..CH15    unwired                -           within noise

  A MUX CHANNEL SWITCHES A HI/LO PAIR, so one die costs ONE channel here -
  unlike probe03's form C card, where a die costs two. That is what makes the
  four channels line up one-for-one with LaMP's fldSwitch 1,2,3,4.  [MEASURED]

  THE ANALOG BUS IS NOT IN THE PATH. The Keithley 2400 is on the card's own
  terminals via rear triax. Closing tree switch 90, 91 or 92 - alone, or
  together with a wired channel - moved the reading by less than the noise.
  So the AT/BT/AT2 tree switches are irrelevant on this bench and should be
  left open.                                                      [MEASURED]

  HOW IT WAS MEASURED (2026-08-10, scrap wafer, pins in contact):
    Keithley 2400 sourcing 10 V into a 1 uA compliance - 10 uW worst case,
    which is why this was safe to sweep blind. Each channel was closed and
    read against an ALL-OPEN reference taken IMMEDIATELY BEFORE IT, then the
    whole sweep repeated in reverse order. CH00-CH03 came out positive in
    both directions and far outside the +-40 pA noise band; every other
    channel sat inside it and six of them CHANGED SIGN between passes.
    That sign flip is the tell - see section 10.

  OPERATING RULE: exactly one of CH00-CH03 closed at a time. Nothing here can
  be damaged by closing two - a mux channel carries a whole pair, so there is
  no pin-to-pin short of the kind probe03 can produce - but two closed
  channels put two dies in PARALLEL, and the reading is then the pair, not
  the die.

  SETTLING - THE PART LaMP GOT WRONG. This path has a time constant of
  several seconds (RC: roughly 10 GOhm against ~1 nF of probe card and
  cabling). A reading taken straight after switching is NOT settled.
  LaMP's own production database shows the consequence:      [MEASURED]

      switch 1 : mean -1.1449e-09 A     <-- +97 pA less negative
      switch 2 : mean -1.2330e-09 A
      switch 3 : mean -1.2439e-09 A
      switch 4 : mean -1.2474e-09 A          (n = 60 quads each)

  Switch 1 reads systematically high across all 60 quads because it is the
  first reading after the source steps. That is a per-POSITION artifact, not
  a per-die one, and it means LaMP's historical switch-1 numbers are ~8% off.
  Allow >10 s of settling after each switch before reading.

  TYPICAL RESULT on the scrap wafer used: ~-1.0 nA at 10 V, i.e. 10 GOhm,
  with each die adding 65-99 GOhm on top of a -700 pA instrument offset.
  For comparison LaMP's own logged readings are -1.00 to -1.34 nA at 10 V,
  so this reproduces the original system's numbers.                [MEASURED]


===============================================================================
 9.  WHAT IS NOT ESTABLISHED
===============================================================================

  - Which physical die of the 2x2 quad is quad position 1, 2, 3 or 4.
    The slash-separated device ID is now known to be COLUMN-major -
    TL / BL / TR / BR (confirmed on a real align site, 54-00/44-70/54-01/
    44-71) - so "die 1..4" here means the 1st..4th entry of that string. What
    is NOT established is that relay CH00 addresses the FIRST entry rather
    than some other corner.                                        [INFERRED]

    An attempt to settle it from LaMP's production data FAILED and is worth
    recording so nobody repeats it: if switch N addressed entry N, a switch
    pointing at an 'NA' position should read like an open circuit. It does
    not - NA rows mean -1.166e-09 A against -1.221e-09 A for real dies,
    statistically indistinguishable. The reason is now obvious from the
    numbers above: a connected die only shifts the reading ~150 pA on a
    ~700 pA offset, so "connected" versus "open" is a ~15% change buried in
    the spread. The isolation test simply is not sensitive enough to
    self-identify which corner it is looking at.                   [MEASURED]

    To settle it you need an asymmetry: a wafer with a known-different
    structure at one corner, or a continuity check from a named pad.

  - The strap question in section 8 (probe03 only).

  - Whether LaMP used 2-wire or 4-wire is now ANSWERED, not open: strings in
    the exe include "syst:rsen off", i.e. 2-wire. It also sent
    "rout:term rear" and "syst:guar cabl".                          [MEASURED]


===============================================================================
 10. WAYS TO FOOL YOURSELF  (READ THIS ONE)
===============================================================================

  (a) A FLOATING INPUT DRIFTS, AND THE DRIFT LOOKS LIKE SWITCHING.

      Measuring the E1326B while changing multiplexer state produced readings
      that changed every time - apparently proving the analog bus worked.
      A control run of the SAME state, repeated, gave:

          6516 -> 8337 -> 8203 -> 7161 -> 5834 ohm

      and interleaving two states decayed straight through both, into
      NEGATIVE resistance. It was the meter's input settling with nothing
      connected. The first reading after a reset was 9.9e37 - overload, i.e.
      open, the honest answer.

      The analog bus cable is NOT fitted on probe02.                 [MEASURED]

      ALWAYS run the repeat-the-same-state control before believing that a
      relay changed anything.

  (b) A CHANNEL WITHOUT ITS TREE SWITCH CONNECTS NOTHING.
      Section 4. Easy to miss; produces a clean, confident open circuit.

  (c) FORM C RELAYS LATCH.
      Section 5. The card powers up however it was left. Send *RST before
      trusting any state.

  (d) ALL THREE CARDS ANSWER *IDN? IDENTICALLY.
      Only SYST:CTYP? distinguishes them, and the mapping from address to
      card type differs between benches.

  (e) DO NOT WRITE 0 TO A FAILED MODULE'S CONTROL REGISTER.
      On probe03, VXI:WRITE to the failed E1326B's control register bit 1
      restored its ability to drive SYSFAIL. The Resource Manager had
      disabled that deliberately to hold the failed card off-line. The whole
      mainframe hung, and being mid-chain on GPIB it took the prober and the
      3458A down with it. Recovery was a power cycle.                [MEASURED]

  (f) A COLD GPIB OPEN CAN FAULT INSIDE THE VENDOR DLL.
      The first open of an address needs longer than 500 ms and fails with an
      access violation rather than a clean error; afterwards 500 ms is fine
      forever. instruments/gpib_base.py now retries at 2500 ms.       [MEASURED]

  (g) READING BEFORE THE RC HAS SETTLED. Same family of error as (a), and it
      caught the probe02 mapping twice.

      The measurement path is ~10 GOhm against ~1 nF, so several seconds.
      A first attempt used 0.35 s per channel and concluded "no channel
      changes anything" - which proved nothing, because nothing had settled.
      A later I-V sweep at 0.8 s per point produced a beautifully linear fit
      giving 4.26 GOhm; that number was also meaningless. The tell was in the
      data: the MEASURED voltages lagged the COMMANDED ones by a gap that
      grew to ~6 V. Always read back the source value, not just the current -
      if V has not arrived, neither has I.                          [MEASURED]

  (h) PEAK-OF-N-NOISY-SAMPLES IS NOT A SIGNAL.
      An attempt to find connected channels quickly by looking for a
      charging-current spike at NPLC 0.01 flagged 8 of 16 channels. It was
      comparing the peak of 12 noisy fast samples against a quiet NPLC 10
      baseline, so the "ratio" mostly measured fast-mode noise. The traces
      gave it away - a real charging transient decays monotonically in ONE
      direction, and these swung bipolar sample to sample.           [MEASURED]

  (i) DRIFT IN A SWEEP LOOKS LIKE A GROUPING.
      Sweeping channels in order and comparing each to ONE baseline taken at
      the start attributes the drift since then to whatever channel is being
      measured, producing a tidy-looking trend. Take the reference
      IMMEDIATELY BEFORE each channel, and sweep in both directions: a real
      effect keeps its sign when the order reverses, drift does not. On
      probe02 six channels changed sign between passes - all of them noise.

======================================================================
8C. PROBE CARD -> RELAY, probe02 / LaMP_HP  (2026-08-12)
======================================================================
Established with the operator at the back of the machine, plus the
measurements in 8B. Cards, read from the instruments themselves via
SYST:CTYP? 1 rather than from the labels:

    GPIB0::9::15   LADDR 120   E1345A   <- the card in use
    GPIB0::9::10   LADDR  80   (E1364A per instruments.yaml; did not
                                answer on 2026-08-12, retry)
    GPIB0::9::14   LADDR 112   E1364A

The operator read the top card's label as E1343A; the card reports
E1345A. Same family - 16 channels, same numbering, same tree topology,
same 100 ohm series protection - so nothing about the wiring changes,
but SYST:CTYP? is the authority and the label is not.

EIGHT wires run from the probe card to the top card, landing on the
CH0..CH3 HI/LO terminals. Four channels x a HI/LO pair is exactly eight,
and each LaMP die has exactly two pads, so the count corroborates the
model rather than merely fitting it.

  channel   HI          LO          die (quad corner)   status
  CH00      A32 TLU     A33 TLD     TL  = switch 1      die: MEASURED
  CH01      A34 BLU     A36 BLD     BL  = switch 2      HI/LO: INFERRED
  CH02      A13 TRU     A12 TRD     TR  = switch 3
  CH03      A11 BRU     A9  BRD     BR  = switch 4

CHANNEL -> DIE IS MEASURED, twice, on TARGET/41-71/TARGET/41-72: the
TARGET positions are solid metal and clamp at compliance, the two real
dies do not, and that pattern matched the original exe's own export 4
times out of 4 both times.

HI vs LO WITHIN A PAIR IS INFERRED, not measured, and cannot be measured
from the instrument side. The pad pair is symmetric - at +10 V and -10 V
a real die read +1.99 nA and -2.22 nA, agreeing within 10% - so swapping
HI and LO produces the same magnitudes with flipped signs and there is no
handedness to detect. The inference is from the pin ordering: taking the
channels in order the pins run A32,A33 | A34,A36 up one side of the card
and A13,A12 | A11,A9 back down the other, and in every pair the U pad
comes first. If the harness was built pair-by-pair with the first wire on
HI then U is HI throughout, which is the only self-consistent reading.
Settle it with a continuity check from the 2400's rear HI to A32 vs A33.

Nothing depends on it: the verdict is short-vs-not and the pair is
symmetric. It matters only as a record of which needle carried the signal.

THE KEITHLEY 2400 lands on the card's DIRECT voltage-sense HI/LO
terminals - the bank common - not the analog bus. That is why the tree
switch sweep in 8B found 90/91/92 made no difference: the trees gate the
bank onto the VXI analog bus, which nothing on this bench uses. An
oddity in 8B, explained.

TWO PINS PER PAD on this card (A-side and B-side, e.g. A32 and B32 both
on TLU). Only the A-side is landed. Per the operator these are spare
needles the manufacturer fitted, NOT a Kelvin force/sense pair - an
earlier reading of the pinout as 4-wire-capable was wrong. This is
specific to LaMP_HP and must not be assumed of other cards.

THE 3458A is not connected to the probe card on probe02. Four direct
wires exist for a probe03-style 4-wire hookup but are not landed.

Ways to fool yourself, continued:
  (j) Reading the card type off the label. Two of the three cards here
      are visually similar and the one in use reports a different model
      than its label was read as.
  (k) Concluding a polarity from a sign difference. This bench reads
      +2 nA where the original logged -0.5 nA, which looks like reversed
      HI/LO until the arithmetic is done: neither orientation reproduces
      the original's sign, because both numbers are the respective
      bench's own leakage floor rather than the die.


======================================================================
12. SOFTWARE SIDE: WHAT ACTUALLY CLOSES WHEN A RECIPE STEP RUNS
    (compiled 2026-09-08, from gui/instrument_panel.py,
    gui/eg_pma_run_panel.py, gui/recipe_panel.py, instruments/
    hp_switchbox.py - triggered by "how does the recipe know what
    pins to close, it has no conn shown")
======================================================================

Sections 1-11 above are the HARDWARE. This section is the SOFTWARE path
a recipe step actually takes when Run/Measure is pressed - three
different mechanisms exist, only one of which is wired into the live
run engine.

--- 12.1  THREE WAYS A STEP CAN NAME A CHANNEL, ONE OF THEM DEAD -------

  (a) step["conn"] - a literal, hand-typed or auto-computed text field on
      the step. generic instrument_panel.py step loop: if conn is
      non-blank, closes exactly those channels via switch.close_channel()
      before taking the reading; if conn is BLANK, closes nothing at
      all - the loop `for ch in chans: ...` simply has nothing to
      iterate.                                              [MEASURED, code]

  (b) hp_switchbox.HPSwitchbox.route_die(die) - closes a die's whole
      DIE_SETS[die] channel set in one call (open_all + close each).
      Exists, correct, but ONLY ever called from switchbox_test_panel.py
      (the manual debug/test tab). NOT called from instrument_panel.py's
      run loop, NOT called from eg_pma_run_panel.py. A real recipe run
      never reaches this function.                          [MEASURED, code]

  (c) recipe_panel.py's "Compute Connection" / "Recompute Connections"
      buttons - derive step["conn"] FROM the step's own fields and write
      it back, so (a) has something correct to close. Accretech derives
      it from the step's hi/lo PIN NAMES against Switch Settings'
      crosspoint topology. Electroglas is different (see 12.2): it
      derives conn from the step's DIE NUMBER via hp_switchbox.
      BENCH_WIRING, NOT from hi/lo at all - this is
      RecipePanel._step_connections_eg(), and it already existed before
      this investigation.                                   [MEASURED, code]

  So: what actually routes anything during a real run is (a) alone - a
  plain text field that has to already be correct before Run is pressed.
  (b) is unreachable dead code. (c) is the tool that's supposed to keep
  (a) correct, and on Electroglas it was already fully built, just never
  connected to the recipe's hi/lo fields for display/validation (12.3).

--- 12.2  hp_switchbox.BENCH_WIRING IS THE SAME TABLE FOR ALL THREE -----

  bench_wiring(name)["die_sets"] is one dict, read by:
    - route_die() (12.1b, dead)
    - RecipePanel._step_connections_eg() (12.1c, the Compute/Recompute
      buttons and the Connections viewer panel)
  and is the CODE FORM of the wiring measured in sections 8B/8C above.
  "probe02" and "Probe03New" (the bench name after the 2026-09-07 card
  move - see the comment above BENCH_WIRING["Probe03New"] in
  hp_switchbox.py) carry the IDENTICAL die_sets: {1:(0,), 2:(1,), 3:(2,),
  4:(3,)} - die N closes channel N-1, zero-padded to "00".."03".

--- 12.3  WHAT WAS FOUND ON THE ACTUAL RECIPE (LaMP_HP, LaMP_HP.recipes.
          electroglas.csv, checked 2026-09-08) --------------------------

  Three LaMP-family recipes exist for Electroglas on this card. They are
  NOT equivalent and do NOT all route:

    "HP LaMP electrical gauge"  bench "Probe03New"  instrument SMU
        conn = 00 / 01 / 02 / 03 for Die 1/2/3/4's Bias Voltage and
        Leakage Measurement steps.  MATCHES bench_wiring("Probe03New")

        hi/lo were BLANK on every step before this fix (12.4) - conn was
        hand-typed correctly, but nothing tied it back to the probe
        card's own PIN table or to Switch Settings for display/

    "Lamp 21PCM whole wafer"  bench "probe03"  instrument DMM
        conn is BLANK on every step (Leakage Measurement, Bias Voltage).

        WITH A SCOPE BEFORE TRUSTING A READING FROM THIS RECIPE]

    "HPLaMP_WHOLE_WAFER"  (lives in the separate LaMP_HP.csv main file,
